# Check what context selection removes

I will test fifteen controlled subject patterns on the complete inventories from nine inspected recordings. Each pattern has nine examples, with service order counterbalanced. Full-note, full-prefix and latest-service excerpt inputs share one literal subject question and three rounds. The budget is 1,215 unconditional calls and answers.

The new notes and references are frozen before inspecting the excerpt comparison's results. They cover direct names, ordinary pronouns, request antecedents, negation, explicit service changes, quoted/requested/question interruptions, ambiguous pairs, missing subjects, later clarification, outside-inventory subjects, quoted and compound targets, and later distractors. These are assistant-written examples, not authentic reports, fresh incidents or independently reviewed labels.

Two references distinguish the subject identified by the complete note from the subject justified by each supplied context. When truncation removes the only disambiguating statement, unresolved is correct for that reduced input. A lucky guess matching the complete note still counts as unsupported. Losing available information remains a coverage loss even when withholding is appropriate.

Eligibility means only a resolved subject with probability at least 0.70. No numerical verdict or analyst recommendation is produced. Results will retain full denominators, confident wrong or unsupported resolutions, ambiguity handling, coverage losses, quarantines and costs. The excerpt remains the declared candidate; passing unlocks no protected recording or operational deployment.
