# Assistant review of the controlled notes

I can review the service and bounded meaning in these notes without asking a person to decide every sentence. The user selected that path on 2026-10-05. This is an assistant-reviewed diagnostic; it does not establish equivalence to a human analyst or measure human review effort.

## Review provenance

The same assistant wrote the controlled notes, helped define their annotations and inspected earlier failures. This review is neither independent nor blinded. Decisions were made from the complete note text and committed before reopening the stored references in this continuation. That ordering prevents post-reference repair within this experiment; it does not erase earlier exposure.

All 27 notes on nine inspected recordings receive a complete twelve-sentence review. The committed artifact preserves each exact sentence, final decision, original round-one family-definition proposal and review rationale. A mechanical expansion writes explicitly selected per-note services, channels, duration measures, polarity and block positions; it does not infer those choices or copy a reference answer.

Questions, requests, headings and explicitly unendorsed allegations are withheld. Compounds remain unsplit; the final either/or service claim remains unresolved. Endorsed single health and cause claims are confirmed as meanings, while their evidence verdict remains a separate task. No fabricated model probability or review time enters the decisions.

## Experiment setup

Both inputs use the same assistant-reviewed claims and unchanged facts. Bound text supplies the exact sentence and reviewed service, using the earlier frozen request builder. Explicit meaning also supplies the reviewed assertion family, channel or duration measure and polarity. Its wording identifies the reviewer as an assistant. This is a question-wording package, so any difference cannot be attributed solely to the family field.

The budget allows 162 once-only calls: 27 notes, two inputs and three rounds, with at most 972 answers. Reviewed bindings must agree with existing annotations before exact requests freeze. Any mismatch stops this plan without rewriting the decisions. Each application, wording and round must meet the frozen verdict, display and complete-note requirements; explicit meaning may lose no correct verdict or display against contemporary bound text. The 0.70 verdict display boundary stays fixed.

The experiment opens no fresh measurements or protected recordings. Its results will describe Jev after same-author review on controlled notes, not automatic free-text extraction, authentic reports, specialist judgment or operational readiness. The original [human review workflow](claim-review.md) remains available for a later study of actual analyst behavior.

## Results

All 162 calls completed, returning 972 valid verdicts without quarantined answers. Both inputs judged and displayed every reviewed claim correctly in every round. Explicit meaning passes the predeclared controlled comparison, with no accuracy or display gain against bound text.

Every plain, negated and boundary panel also passes. Each input displays all six verdicts for every note throughout. Health and causal assertions still receive unanswerable where the policy cannot establish them; a correct judgment does not prove health or cause.

The committed review agrees with all existing routable annotations. It confirms 18 claims withheld by the first-round family-definition extractor: three in Train Ticket and fifteen in Online Boutique. Four relevant bindings change. Six other confirmed sentences change only irrelevant fields, such as clearing a channel for a non-metric assertion. None of these counts measures human correction burden.

Bound text used 81 calls, 332,187 input tokens and 15.56 summed request seconds. Explicit meaning used 81 calls, 358,953 tokens and 16.39 seconds. Its extra 26,766 input tokens, about 8.1%, bought no observed accuracy or display improvement. These recorded latency sums are not service guarantees.

## What this says about Jev

Jev works well here as a bounded evidence checker after explicit claim and service assignment. The simpler bound-text request remains the preferred input on this evidence: it matches explicit meaning with fewer tokens. This supports continued learning without waiting for human sentence decisions, provided the study keeps same-assistant review visible.

It does not establish that the assistant matches an independent operations analyst, that Jev can reliably parse unrestricted incident notes, or that either is ready for telecom automation. Both review and references share an author, the recordings are already inspected, and the text uses a small known taxonomy.

## Next experiment

The next useful automatic test is sensitivity to incorrect bindings: hold the exact sentence and facts fixed, introduce controlled errors in subject or meaning, and measure whether Jev follows those errors or retains the source sentence's meaning. That would clarify the value and risk of passing normalized fields. A separate frozen protocol must preserve the clean controls, mark corrupted inputs explicitly and score verdict changes against the original claims. It needs no human decisions or protected recordings.

Independent analyst review remains useful later for authentic language, unfamiliar network conventions and disagreement about policy. That is a different validation task from this controlled diagnostic.

The subsequent [binding-corruption diagnostic](binding-corruption.md) tests that dependency with deliberately incorrect subject and polarity fields. Its clean controls remain correct, while wrong-subject pronoun claims produce confident errors. The evidence supports explicit subject checking as the next comparison.
