# Does Jev add useful clarification beyond rules?

This comparison asks whether Jev identifies a useful next question more reliably than a fixed literal rule baseline. Its 48 new controlled statements reuse two inspected public service catalogs. They supply no measurements and open no additional recordings.

## Setup

Twelve pairs per application cover complete wording, explicit omissions and subtler language: competing antecedents, a resolved former/latter reference, metric alternatives, a replacement claim, contradictory assertions and another time period. Two pairs preserve complete wording while changing its presentation. The paired references are declared before inference. These are assistant-authored statements in known semantic families, not independent reports or fresh incidents.

The rules recognize observed names, supplied CPU/memory aliases, magnitude and direction expressions, final-correction markers, conflicting assertions and explicit windows. They use no fitted parameters or model answers. They have limited language coverage; every unnecessary question remains in the comparison.

Jev receives the unchanged focused six-field request used in the earlier optional-question study. Code selects its first clarification in service, metric, comparison, assertion and window order. The fixed 0.70 display boundary and whole-statement malformed-field rejection remain intact. No result selects a field, declares readiness or computes a numerical verdict.

The plan and producers commit before preparing text files. A separate exact protocol commits before 144 once-only calls, covering all 48 statements in three rounds. There are no retries, post-result rule changes, text repairs or threshold searches. The rule baseline reads those same statements without provider calls.

## What would justify another walkthrough?

Every application and round must meet all of these criteria:

- Structurally valid answers for every statement.
- No unnecessary or wrong-order displayed question, silent ambiguity miss or wrong scope.
- At least 90% correct first-question coverage when a question is needed, overall and separately for explicit omissions and subtle language.
- At least as many correct displayed actions as rules overall.
- At least two correct-display gains over rules in subtle language, with no paired losses in that category.

These are research criteria, not an operational reliability standard. Passing would justify another usability comparison; it would not establish analyst benefit. A failed candidate remains failed, even if one category or the rule control performs well.

## Interface changes

A separately versioned learning sandbox will preserve the original walkthrough and reuse its known examples. It will label the source buttons as scripted replies from a simulated report author, use “metric” instead of “metric channel,” and distinguish a clarification request from confirmation of an initial choice. Withheld suggestions will have a neutral unavailable status. A missing suggestion never clears a claim.

The sandbox is unscored practice, not a second participant experiment. Its local exports use a separate format and cannot enter the original walkthrough's assessment. Participant records and comments remain private and outside public releases.

## Status

The experiment is planned and unexecuted. All text, reference masks, literal rules, instructions, boundaries and research criteria freeze before requests. Exact numerical checks remain in code; no new source access is needed.
